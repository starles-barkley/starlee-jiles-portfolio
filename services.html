Services

A few starting points — not a rigid menu

Most projects fall into one of these three general shapes. Think of them as starting points for a conversation, not a fixed catalog — what you actually need may borrow from more than one.

The features listed below are examples. We'll decide what makes sense for your project based on what you actually need.

Option One

A Simple Website

A good fit for a professional, small business, service provider, ministry, event, or personal brand that needs a clear, trustworthy presence online.

Possible features we could include, depending on what you need:

  • Home, About, and Services information
  • Contact form
  • Hours and location
  • Photo gallery
  • Mobile-friendly design
  • Links to social media
  • Basic updates and maintenance

Option Two

A More Advanced Website

For a business or organization that needs more than an informational site — something that does more work for you and your visitors.

Possible features we could include, depending on what you need:

  • Multiple pages
  • Appointment or inquiry workflows
  • Frequently updated content
  • Blog or news sections
  • Customer forms
  • Search or filtering
  • Connections to other tools or services you already use
  • More customized functionality specific to your business

Option Three

Something Different

Not every problem is a website. If you have a repetitive administrative task, a document process that eats up time, an information-management headache, or an idea that doesn't fit a normal category, that's worth a conversation too.

As an example of how I approach this kind of problem: earlier in my career, I spent time as a legal assistant and saw firsthand how much repetitive manual work went into producing legal documents. After moving into software development, I used that experience to design and build document-generation software for a law firm, which I still maintain today as a contract software engineer. That application is private to the firm — it's not something I can show visitors — but it reflects the way I think about this kind of work: understand the real process first, then build something that fits it.

Not sure which of these fits?

That's completely normal, and you don't need to figure it out before reaching out. Tell me what you do, what's frustrating you, or what you wish worked more easily, and I can help figure out what kind of solution actually makes sense — whether that's a website, a tool, or something in between.
